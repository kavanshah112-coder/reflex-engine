# V1.6 — The Scientific Reflex Engine

**Reflex Engine, experiment V1.6 · OUTCOME B · 2026-09-03**

Re-scoped to its proven regime and allowed to grow a library, the
engine did the thing three versions of narrative work could not:
**118,000+ sealed serves across six arms and two sealed sets with
zero wrong-fire**, foreign-phrased in-domain coverage of **55.5%**
against a pre-registered 40% bar, and a four-model minted reflex
library that lifted sealed coverage **+24.6 points** without a single
wrong answer attributable to any minted reflex. The one component
that failed its bar — honestly, on all four scales — is the verifier,
and the frozen ladder prices that at rung B.

## 3. The depth ladder (M-all, sealed) — NOT a decay curve

The pre-registered guess was a decay. The measurement is sharper and
non-monotonic, and wrong-fire is zero at every rung:

```
depth   M-all served / pool     coverage   wrong
d1      10,429 / 11,915          87.5%      0
d2       4,301 /  8,594          50.0%      0
d3       5,602 /  5,617          99.7%      0
d4+          0 /  2,777           0.0%      0
dist         0 /  1,097 served    0.0%      0   (all escalated/abstained)
```

Depth is not the variable that ends coverage — SHAPE is. d3's
cross-domain chains (a derivative feeding a unit conversion, an
energy feeding kilojoules, revenue feeding a margin) are
template-regular, and minted reflexes plus the unit-honoring gateway
nearly saturated them. d2's mid-question chains ("…then multiply the
result by 7") sit at 50% because half their phrasings hide the chain
in clause structure no admitted pattern owns. d4+ is zero BY GUARD:
those items end in a comparison, the choice-ask guard refuses to
answer a comparison with a number, and no minter proposed (and one
explicitly declined to propose) a reflex that could — so the whole
rung escalates safely. The edge of certified calculation is a
measured shape, not a depth.

## 4. Phase L — reflex creation, watched (the per-model ledger)

Same 1,500 escalated residuals offered to every model through the
isolated bridge; identical gauntlet (G1 schema / G2 evidence-based
taxonomy placement / G3 shadow probation: ≥3 serves, ≥2 distinct
operand tuples, ZERO wrong):

| model | proposed | G2 killed | inert | **wrong-kills** | admitted | sealed foreign lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| haiku | 139 | 22 | 39 | **16** | 62 | +8.9pp |
| sonnet | 107 | 10 | 31 | **3** | 63 | +11.2pp |
| opus | 111 | 14 | 20 | **1** | 76 | +16.6pp |
| fable | 102 | 9 | 22 | **1** | 70 | +13.7pp |
| **union (M-all)** | 271 | 0 | 0 | **0** | **271** | **+20.2pp** |

**V5 falsified in the good direction.** V1.1's scale inversion (opus
minted MORE wrong) did not recur: probation wrong-kills fall
monotonically with scale (16 → 3 → 1 → 1) and sealed lift rises.
The difference from V1.1 is architectural, not model-side: a V1.6
reflex is a *parse procedure* whose every output passes the
independent witness, so a bigger model's fluency becomes coverage
instead of becoming confident wrong answers. The union re-gauntlet
admitted all 271 with zero cross-model conflicts.

Qualitative ledger notes (preserved verbatim in the mint logs): opus
*declined* to mint the largest residual family (integral-vs-sum
comparisons) because "mapping it would certify a different question,"
and flagged its own g = 9.81 assumption for rejection — the
UNKNOWN > WRONG principle arrived at independently by a minter.
Fable proposed the fewest candidates with the joint-best kill rate.
TEXT.qa-style memorization reflexes were banned outright (a
memorized narrative answer could have opened SAFETY-2); the
anti-memorization gate recorded zero attempts.

**Dev→sealed generalisation held** (dev 52.8% → sealed 55.5%
foreign) — the exact gap that killed V1.5's guards (0% → 40%
wrong-fire) did not appear, because reflexes extend PARSING into a
witness-gated calculator rather than encoding compositions as rules.

## 5. The verifier — a clean, pre-registered failure

Admission ran exactly as frozen. Dev (200 LLM answers/model):
`conflict_present` fired once on a correct answer → voided.
Sealed (400/model, no model answering its own phrasings):
`not_in_text` fired once on a correct answer → voided sealed.
Survivors (`non_integer_count`, `exceeds_total`, and the
never-firing rest) kept **zero false flags** — and caught too little:

| model | LLM wrong on escalated share | caught | recall | bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| haiku | 132/395 (33.4%) | 4 | **3.0%** | ≥10% FAIL |
| sonnet | 48/400 (12.0%) | 3 | **6.3%** | FAIL |
| opus | 45/400 (11.3%) | 3 | **6.7%** | FAIL |
| fable | 46/400 (11.5%) | 3 | **6.5%** | FAIL |

**Why, honestly:** the draft-v1 evidence (8/74 at 0 FF) was measured
on direct-LLM answers over narrative corpora, where errors violate
text-level impossibilities. V1.6's escalated share is the hard
residue of a system that already serves 70% — its LLM errors are
plausible-magnitude computational slips (a wrong definite integral,
a mis-chained d4 composition) that no impossibility check can see.
The verifier transferred its zero-false-flag discipline and did not
transfer its recall. Rung B prices that correctly, and the 11–33%
LLM error rate on the escalated share is itself a finding: what the
engine escalates is precisely what LLMs get wrong at their highest
rates, which is the strongest argument the arc has produced for
serving certified answers wherever coverage reaches.

  parser overreach, requested-unit) were fixed before any counting

## 10. The one-paragraph version

V1.6 stopped asking the engine to read stories and asked instead how
much of its own domain — science, arithmetic, logic — it covers when
someone else does the phrasing, and whether four model scales can
safely grow that coverage into an indexed reflex library. Sealed, at
100k-workload scale: the hand-built engine covers 35.4% of foreign
phrasing, the grown library takes it to 55.5% (70.4% on the mixed
workload), and across 118,000+ served answers in twelve sealed runs
there was not one wrong certificate, one unaddressed certificate, or
one narrative item served. Reflex minting quality improved
monotonically with model scale — V1.1's inversion did not survive an
architecture where producers propose procedures and an independent
witness checks every output. The verifier failed its recall bar on
all four scales exactly as pre-registered checks are allowed to fail,
which fixes the outcome at B rather than A and leaves the arc with a
sharp next question: on an escalated share this hard, catching LLM
errors needs recomputation, not impossibility checks — and
recomputation is what the engine already does on the 70% it serves.
